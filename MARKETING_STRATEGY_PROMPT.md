# KinRead Marketing Strategy Prompt

---

You are a senior growth strategist for an early-stage consumer subscription product. Build a practical marketing strategy for **KinRead**, a web app that helps parents decide whether a children's or YA book is right for their family before they say yes.

## Product context

KinRead lets a parent:
- Search for a book by title, ISBN/barcode, or cover photo.
- Receive an AI-assisted content analysis covering sexual content, language, violence/scary content, substance use, self-harm/suicide, and other themes.
- See plain-English age guidance, a family-specific "Good to go", "Worth discussing first", or "Not a fit for this family" verdict for each child, and conversation starters.
- Save books to a private library and set content boundaries for each child.
- Search for books by theme, subject, genre, or mental-model lesson.

The product is called KinRead. Its core promise is: **"Know the book before you say yes."**

The product currently has:
- Email/password accounts and private family data.
- A free tier of five new analyses per month; previously analysed books can be served from a shared cache without consuming a new analysis.
- A planned paid family tier at approximately US$5.99/month. Stripe billing infrastructure exists but the live paid checkout has not yet been activated.
- A responsive web app, not a native mobile app.
- A small pre-launch budget and no established audience yet.
- A New Zealand-based founder, but the product can initially serve English-speaking parents internationally.

## Constraints and principles

- Do not invent traction, testimonials, partnerships, performance figures, legal compliance, or product features.
- Treat AI-generated content guidance as decision support, not a guarantee or replacement for parent judgment.
- Avoid manipulative fear-based marketing about children's books.
- Protect parent and child privacy. Do not recommend collecting unnecessary child data or using child-targeted advertising.
- Prioritise low-cost, ethical, testable channels suitable for a solo founder.
- Assume the founder can spend 5-10 hours per week on marketing for the next 90 days.
- Be concrete. Recommend only activities that could realistically be run now or with a clearly identified prerequisite.

## Your job

First, state the 5 most important assumptions you are making. Then create a phased, evidence-led go-to-market plan that starts before paid billing is live and can convert into a paid subscription strategy later.

Deliver the strategy in the following format:

1. **Positioning**
   - One primary customer segment to focus on first, plus two secondary segments.
   - Jobs-to-be-done, key anxieties, desired outcomes, and why KinRead is distinct from Common Sense Media, Goodreads, publisher age ranges, and general-purpose AI chatbots.
   - A concise positioning statement, a one-sentence elevator pitch, and three message pillars with proof points.

2. **Offer and funnel**
   - Recommend the best initial acquisition offer and activation goal.
   - Map the journey from first discovery through account creation, first analysis, saved library, repeat use, referral, and eventual paid upgrade.
   - Identify the highest-leverage activation event and 3 retention loops.
   - Specify where the free tier should be mentioned, and where it should not distract a new visitor.

3. **Channel strategy**
   - Rank 6 channels by expected impact, effort, cost, speed to learning, and risk.
   - Include a balanced mix of organic search/content, parent communities, librarian/educator relationships, partnerships, email, and one carefully scoped paid experiment.
   - For each of the top 3 channels, provide: audience, exact angle, 3 sample campaign or content ideas, distribution approach, CTA, measurement, and a stop/continue decision rule.
   - Call out channels to avoid in the first 90 days and explain why.

4. **90-day execution plan**
   - Provide a week-by-week plan for weeks 1-4, then monthly plans for months 2 and 3.
   - Keep weekly work within 5-10 founder hours.
   - Include the smallest viable marketing assets to create first: landing-page copy, a short demo, email capture or onboarding emails, a launch post, and one useful recurring content format.
   - Clearly label dependencies, especially payment activation, analytics, email tooling, and any landing-page work.

5. **Experiments and measurement**
   - Propose 8 prioritised experiments with hypothesis, audience, asset, success metric, minimum sample or observation window, and decision threshold.
   - Define a simple weekly dashboard: acquisition, activation, engagement, retention, conversion, referral, and qualitative learning metrics.
   - Recommend privacy-conscious analytics events. Do not use book titles or child names as personally identifying analytics dimensions.

6. **Copy starter pack**
   - Homepage headline/subheadline/CTA options: 5 sets.
   - 5 short social posts, tailored separately to Facebook parent groups, Instagram, TikTok/Reels, and LinkedIn where appropriate.
   - A respectful outreach email to a school librarian or parent community administrator.
   - A 3-email welcome sequence for new free accounts.

7. **Risks and decisions**
   - Identify the 5 biggest marketing risks or positioning traps.
   - Recommend the next 3 product or operational decisions the founder should make before spending meaningful money on acquisition.
   - End with a single prioritised "do this next Monday" checklist of no more than 8 items.

Use tables where they make comparison easier. Distinguish facts from recommendations. If key information is missing, ask no more than 5 targeted questions at the end, but still give the best complete strategy you can with the information above.
